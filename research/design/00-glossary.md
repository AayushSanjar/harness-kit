## 1. Visual design basics

- **Alignment.** Placing the edges or centres of elements on shared invisible lines, so the layout looks ordered. *Example:* the left edges of a form's labels and fields all sit at the same x position. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Breakpoint.** A screen width at which a layout changes its arrangement. *Example:* Atlassian's grid switches from 6 to 12 columns at 1024 pixels. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/grid | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Clipping.** Content cut off at the edge of its container, so part of it cannot be seen. *Example:* the last letters of a long label hidden behind a fixed-width box. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Coherence.** The sense that all parts of a design belong together, through shared colours, type, spacing and style. *Example:* Anthropic's "design quality" criterion asks whether a design feels like a coherent whole. [VERIFIED | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]
- **Corner radius.** How rounded an element's corners are, in pixels. *Example:* Atlassian gives buttons and inputs a 6-pixel radius. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/radius | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Density (information density).** How much information a screen shows in a given space. *Example:* a compact table with 30 rows per screen against a comfortable one with 12. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Eight-point grid.** A convention that all spacing is a multiple of 8 pixels, sometimes 4. *Example:* archived Material Design 1 aligned components to an 8 dp grid, where dp is Android's density-independent pixel. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | m1.material.io/layout/metrics-keylines.html | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Elevation.** The visual suggestion that one surface sits above another, shown with shadows and lighter or darker backgrounds. *Example:* a dropdown menu with a shadow floating over the page. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/elevation | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Ellipsis truncation.** Shortening text that does not fit and ending it with "…". *Example:* "Quarterly report for the finance…" in a narrow column. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Fallback font.** The font a browser uses when the intended font is missing or not yet loaded. *Example:* a system sans-serif font shown instead of Atlassian Sans. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Font family and typeface.** A typeface is a designed set of letter shapes. A font family is the name a web page asks for, followed by fallbacks. *Example:* "Atlassian Sans", then ui-sans-serif and other system fonts. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@atlaskit/tokens@20.0.0/dist/esm/artifacts/tokens-raw/atlassian-typography.js | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Font weight.** How thick the strokes of the letters are, as a number where 400 is regular and 700 is bold. *Example:* Atlassian headings use weight 653. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@atlaskit/tokens@20.0.0/dist/esm/artifacts/tokens-raw/atlassian-typography.js | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Grid (layout grid).** Columns with fixed gaps (gutters) and outer margins that content is placed on. *Example:* 12 columns with 16-pixel gutters on a wide screen. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/grid | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Line height (leading).** The vertical distance from one line of text to the next. *Example:* 14-pixel text on a 20-pixel line height. [VERIFIED | atlassian.design/foundations/typography | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Line length (measure).** The number of characters on one line of text. *Example:* GOV.UK recommends no more than 75 characters per line. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/layout | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Modular scale.** A series of font sizes where each size is the previous one multiplied by a fixed ratio. *Example:* Atlassian's scale steps by 1.2 from a 16-pixel base. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/typography/product-typefaces-and-scale | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Native fit.** How much an embedded component looks like part of the product that hosts it. *Example:* a Jira gadget using Jira's fonts, colours and spacing. [ASSUMPTION: my term in these files]
- **Originality.** Evidence of deliberate, custom design choices rather than templates and defaults. *Example:* one of the four criteria in Anthropic's design harness. [VERIFIED | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]
- **Overflow.** Content larger than its container, which then spills out or needs scrolling. *Example:* a table wider than its panel. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Overlap.** Two elements drawn on top of each other when they should not be. *Example:* a legend covering the last data points of a chart. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Pseudo-localisation.** Replacing interface text with longer, accented versions to reveal layout faults before real translation. *Example:* "Save" becomes "[Šåvé ~~~]". [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | learn.microsoft.com/en-us/globalization/methodology/pseudolocalization | 2022-08-12]
- **Reflow.** Content rearranging itself to fit a narrow screen, so that it can be read by scrolling in one direction only. *Example:* two side-by-side columns stacking into one at 320 pixels wide. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html | 2026-08-10]
- **Responsive design.** Design that adapts its layout to the available width. *Example:* a sidebar that collapses into a menu on a phone. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Sentence case.** Capitalising only the first word and proper nouns. *Example:* "Configure cycle time chart", not "Configure Cycle Time Chart". [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/content/language-and-grammar | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Spacing scale.** The fixed set of distances a design is allowed to use for margins, padding and gaps. *Example:* Atlassian's 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64 and 80 pixels. [VERIFIED | atlassian.design/foundations/spacing | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Tabular figures.** Digits that all have the same width, so that numbers line up in columns. *Example:* the CSS setting `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. [VERIFIED | developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-numeric | 2026-09-10]
- **Taste.** A person's preferences about how a design should look and feel, beyond what rules require. *Example:* preferring direct labels on chart lines to a legend. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Theme.** A complete set of colour, and sometimes type and spacing, values that can be switched as a unit. *Example:* Jira's light and dark themes. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/tokens/design-tokens | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Type scale.** The fixed set of font sizes, and their line heights, that a design may use. *Example:* Atlassian's body sizes of 12, 14 and 16 pixels. [VERIFIED | atlassian.design/foundations/typography | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Visual hierarchy.** Making the most important element the most noticeable, through size, weight, colour and position. *Example:* a chart's title larger and bolder than its axis labels. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **White space (negative space).** Empty space around and between elements. *Example:* 24 pixels between a chart and the form below it. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]

## 2. Accessibility

- **ACT rules (Accessibility Conformance Testing rules).** W3C test definitions that several checking tools implement in the same way. *Example:* rule oj04fd passes if at least one pixel changes colour when an element receives focus. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/act/rules/oj04fd | 2023-08-30]
- **APCA (Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm).** An alternative contrast measure that its developer proposes for WCAG 3. The WCAG 3 draft leaves its contrast method undecided. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0 | 2026-09-10] *Example:* APCA sets a minimum lightness contrast of Lc 75 for body text, with Lc 90 preferred. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | readtech.org/ARC/tests/bronze-simple-mode | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **axe-core.** Deque's open-source library of automated accessibility rules, used inside many tools. *Example:* its `color-contrast` rule checks WCAG 1.4.3. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | github.com/dequelabs/axe-core/blob/develop/doc/rule-descriptions.md | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Colour-vision deficiency.** Reduced ability to tell some colours apart, most often red from green. *Example:* about 1 in 12 men have it. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness | 2025-11-05]
- **Conformance levels A, AA and AAA.** WCAG's three levels of strictness. Level A is the least strict, AA is the middle level, and AAA is the strictest. *Example:* 4.5:1 text contrast is AA; 7:1 is AAA. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/TR/WCAG22 | 2024-12-12]
- **Contrast ratio.** WCAG's measure of the difference in brightness between two colours, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white). *Example:* grey #767676 on white is about 4.5:1. [ASSUMPTION: general usage, based on the WCAG definition]
- **Equivalent control.** Another way on the same page to do what a small or hard-to-use control does, which WCAG accepts as an exception to the target-size rule. *Example:* a table listing every item shown as a small dot on a chart. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html | 2026-05-11]
- **Focus indicator (keyboard focus).** The visible mark showing which element will respond to the keyboard. *Example:* a 2-pixel blue outline around a focused button. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/border | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Focus not obscured.** The WCAG rule that the focused element must not be completely hidden by content the page's author created. *Example:* a sticky header covering a focused link fails it. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html | 2026-06-15]
- **Large-scale text.** In WCAG, text of at least 18 point, or 14 point bold, which is about 24 or 18.5 pixels. It needs only 3:1 contrast. *Example:* a 24-pixel heading. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html | 2026-06-01]
- **Lighthouse.** Google's automated page-audit tool, whose accessibility score is built from axe-core rules. *Example:* running a Lighthouse report in Chrome's developer tools. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/accessibility/scoring | 2025-10-22]
- **"Needs review" (incomplete) result.** An automated checker's answer when it cannot decide and a person must look. *Example:* axe cannot compute contrast for text over a gradient. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | github.com/dequelabs/axe-core/blob/develop/locales/_template.json | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Non-text contrast.** The WCAG rule that the parts of controls and graphics needed to understand the content have at least 3:1 contrast with what is next to them. *Example:* a chart line against its background. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html | 2026-06-15]
- **Section 508.** The US federal requirement that government information technology be accessible. *Example:* the US government's Section508.gov site gives typography guidance. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Success criterion.** One testable requirement in WCAG, numbered. *Example:* 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum). [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/TR/WCAG22 | 2024-12-12]
- **Target size.** The clickable or tappable area of a control. WCAG level AA asks for at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels or enough spacing. *Example:* a 16-pixel icon button with no spacing fails. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html | 2026-05-11]
- **Text spacing (WCAG 1.4.12).** The rule that nothing is lost when a user increases line, paragraph, letter and word spacing to set values. *Example:* a button label that wraps and gets cut off fails it. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/TR/WCAG22 | 2024-12-12]
- **WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines).** The W3C standard for web accessibility. Version 2.2 is also the international standard ISO/IEC 40500:2025. *Example:* an audit that tests a page against WCAG 2.2 level AA. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | w3.org/WAI/news/2025-10-21/wcag22-iso | 2025-10-21]

## 3. Rendering and measuring a page

- **Anti-aliasing.** Smoothing jagged edges with in-between colours. It differs between machines, which causes harmless differences between screenshots. *Example:* the grey fringe pixels around black text. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **ARM and x86.** Two families of computer processors. Many laptops use ARM chips and many CI servers use x86 chips, and the same browser can draw slightly different pixels on each. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Bounding box.** The rectangle an element occupies on screen: its x and y position, width and height. *Example:* Playwright's `boundingBox()` returns it relative to the viewport. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | playwright.dev/docs/api/class-locator | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Canvas and SVG.** Two ways a browser can draw graphics. A canvas is a grid of pixels with no elements inside, while SVG draws each shape as an element that scripts can inspect. *Example:* each dot of an SVG scatterplot has its own fill colour that a script can read. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Computed style.** The final value of each CSS property that the browser applies to an element after all rules are combined. *Example:* `font-size: 14px` read with `getComputedStyle`. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/getComputedStyle | 2026-05-11]
- **Continuous integration (CI).** An automated server that builds and tests the code on every change. *Example:* a job that renders every screen and runs the checks on each pull request. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **CSS pixel.** The browser's unit of layout, which may cover several physical pixels on a high-density screen. *Example:* a 24 CSS-pixel target is 48 physical pixels wide on a screen with scale factor 2. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Deterministic rendering.** Rendering that produces exactly the same pixels every time for the same input. *Example:* fonts preloaded, clock fixed and data seeded before each screenshot. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Device scale factor.** The number of physical pixels per CSS pixel. *Example:* Playwright's default is 1. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | playwright.dev/docs/api/class-testoptions | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **DOM (Document Object Model).** The browser's tree of the elements on a page, which scripts can read. *Example:* reading every button element and its label. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Fixture.** A fixed, fake data set used in tests, so that every run shows the same data. *Example:* 60 completed work items over 90 days. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Font hinting and subpixel positioning.** Font-rendering adjustments that differ between operating systems and change letter shapes and positions slightly. *Example:* the Chromium flag `--disable-font-subpixel-positioning`. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/main/ui/gfx/switches.cc | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Headless browser.** A browser running without a visible window, used in automated tests. *Example:* Playwright running Chromium on a CI server. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Playwright.** Microsoft's browser-automation and testing framework. *Example:* `toHaveScreenshot()` compares a page with a stored image. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | playwright.dev/docs/test-snapshots | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Playwright MCP.** A Model Context Protocol server that lets an AI model drive a real browser through Playwright. *Example:* Anthropic's evaluator navigated the live page with it. [VERIFIED | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]
- **Render matrix.** The full set of sizes, themes, states and fixtures that every candidate design is rendered in. One combination of size, theme, state and fixture is called a render-matrix cell. *Example:* three widths, two themes and seven states give 42 cells. [ASSUMPTION: my term in these files]
- **Self-difference test.** Rendering the same screen twice and requiring zero difference, to detect flakiness before comparing designs. *Example:* the harness's rubric line U-20. [ASSUMPTION: my term in these files]
- **Shuffle test.** Rendering a chart with its data rows in a random order and requiring an identical image, to reveal marks that hide each other. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/pdf/2001.02316 | 2020]
- **Viewport.** The visible area of the browser window. *Example:* Playwright's default viewport is 1280 by 720 pixels. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | playwright.dev/docs/api/class-testoptions | accessed 2026-09-27]

## 4. Design systems and the Atlassian platform

- **Allow-list.** The set of values a check accepts. *Example:* the 14 spacing values of Atlassian's scale. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Atlassian Design System (ADS).** Atlassian's published design foundations, tokens, components and content rules, at atlassian.design. *Example:* its data-visualisation colour page. [VERIFIED | atlassian.design/foundations/color/data-visualization-color | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Atlassian Sans and Atlassian Mono.** Atlassian's product fonts, derived from Inter and from JetBrains Mono. *Example:* in-app text uses Atlassian Sans, and code uses Atlassian Mono. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | atlassian.design/foundations/typography/product-typefaces-and-scale | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Codemod.** A tool that rewrites source code automatically. *Example:* Atlassian's `css-to-design-tokens` codemod replaces raw colours with tokens. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | app.unpkg.com/@atlaskit/tokens@20.0.0/files/codemods | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Column span and row span.** How many of the dashboard's 12 columns a Forge widget occupies (3, 4, 6, 8 or 12), and its height class (xsmall, small, medium or large). *Example:* a widget at column span 3 is a quarter of the dashboard's width. [VERIFIED | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/ui-kit/hooks/use-widget-context | 2026-09-14]
- **Configuration screen (edit mode).** The part of a dashboard gadget where the user chooses what it shows. *Example:* choosing a board and a date range. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/manifest-reference/modules/jira-dashboard-gadget | 2025-08-12]
- **Custom UI.** The Forge option in which the app's own HTML and JavaScript run in a frame inside Jira. *Example:* a chart drawn with a third-party library. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/design-tokens-and-theming | 2024-10-30]
- **Dashboard gadget and dashboard widget.** Forge modules that show an app inside a Jira dashboard. The older `jira:dashboardGadget` is deprecated, with removal on 17 May 2027, and `dashboards:widget` replaces it. *Example:* a cycle-time chart placed on a team's dashboard. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.atlassian.com/changelog | entries of 2026-09-22 and 2026-09-23]
- **Design system.** A shared set of design values, components and rules that keeps a product consistent. *Example:* IBM Carbon, GOV.UK Design System, Atlassian Design System. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Design token.** A named design value defined once and referenced everywhere. *Example:* `space.200` = 16 pixels. [VERIFIED | atlassian.design/foundations/spacing | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Developer site (Atlassian).** A free Atlassian cloud site for building and testing apps. *Example:* installing a gadget there to measure its rendered sizes. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **dp and sp.** Android's density-independent pixel (dp), used for sizes, and scale-independent pixel (sp), used for text so that it follows the user's font-size setting. *Example:* a 48 by 48 dp touch target. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **DTCG format.** The token file format of the W3C Design Tokens Community Group, whose first stable version is 2025.10. *Example:* a `tokens.json` file with `$value` entries. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/format | 2025-10-28]
- **Early Access (Atlassian).** Atlassian's label for a feature that is still experimental. The widget hooks page carrying it says the feature is not supported for production use. [VERIFIED | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/ui-kit/hooks/use-widget-context | 2026-09-14]
- **ESLint and Stylelint.** Tools that check JavaScript and CSS source code against rules. *Example:* Atlassian's rule `ensure-design-token-usage` flags hard-coded colours. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@atlaskit/eslint-plugin-design-system/README.md | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Forge.** Atlassian's platform for apps that run inside Jira and Confluence. *Example:* a Forge app adding a gadget to a Jira dashboard. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/design-tokens-and-theming | 2024-10-30]
- **Harness and plugin.** A harness is everything around an AI model that shapes and checks its work: instructions, scripts, agents and tests. A plugin is a packaged bundle of such parts that Claude Code can install. *Example:* this research is for a plugin that checks design quality. [ASSUMPTION: general usage; see `research/00-glossary.md`]
- **Lint rule.** An automated check that reads source code without running it. *Example:* a rule that rejects `color: #FF0000` in favour of a token. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Preset (lint preset).** A ready-made set of lint rules that a project switches on as a unit. *Example:* Atlassian's "recommended" preset. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@atlaskit/eslint-plugin-design-system/README.md | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Productive type set (IBM Carbon).** Carbon's set of type styles for product screens, as opposed to its expressive set for marketing pages. *Example:* body text of 14 pixels on a 20-pixel line. [ASSUMPTION: general usage] for the definition; [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | carbondesignsystem.com/elements/typography/type-sets | 2026-09-23] for the example.
- **Resolved value.** The concrete value a token stands for in the active theme. *Example:* `color.text` resolves to #292A2E in Atlassian's light theme. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@atlaskit/tokens@20.0.0/dist/esm/artifacts/themes/atlassian-light.js | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Style Dictionary.** A tool that converts one token file into CSS and platform formats. *Example:* producing CSS variables from a DTCG file. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | styledictionary.com/info/dtcg | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **UI Kit.** The Forge option in which Atlassian's own components draw the app's screen. *Example:* UI Kit's line chart component. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/ui-kit/components/line-chart | 2026-08-20]
- **`view.theme.enable()`.** The call a Forge Custom UI app makes to follow Jira's light or dark theme. *Example:* without it, a gadget may stay white in dark mode. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/design-tokens-and-theming | 2024-10-30]
- **xcss.** UI Kit's styling method, which allows only token values for colour, spacing, radius and shadow. *Example:* `padding: 'space.200'`. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/ui-kit/components/xcss | 2025-11-24]

## 5. Comparing a page with an approved design

- **ARIA snapshot.** A Playwright check that records the roles, attributes, values and text of a page's accessible elements as a tree, and compares it with a stored text template, not with pixels. *Example:* checking that a Save button still exists and is enabled. [VERIFIED | playwright.dev/docs/aria-snapshots | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Baseline (golden image).** A stored screenshot that a person approved, used as the reference. *Example:* the approved chart at column span 6 in the light theme. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **CIEDE2000 (ΔE).** The modern standard formula for the perceived difference between two colours. *Example:* the looks-same comparison tool treats a CIEDE2000 difference of 2.3 as just noticeable. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | raw.githubusercontent.com/gemini-testing/looks-same/master/lib/constants.js | accessed 2026-09-27] That figure is usually quoted for the older CIE76 formula. [PARTIAL | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_difference | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Figma MCP server.** A server that gives AI models read access to Figma design data. *Example:* reading a frame's colour variables. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | developers.figma.com/docs/figma-mcp-server/tools-and-prompts | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Flaky test.** A test that sometimes passes and sometimes fails with no change to the code. [ASSUMPTION: general usage] *Example:* a screenshot taken before a web font finished loading, which Chromatic's documentation warns about. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | chromatic.com/docs/font-loading | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Just-noticeable difference (JND).** The smallest difference an average viewer can see. *Example:* a ΔE of about 2.3 under the older CIE76 formula. [PARTIAL | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_difference | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Masking.** Hiding a region of a screenshot before comparing, because it legitimately changes. *Example:* covering today's date. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | playwright.dev/docs/api/class-pageassertions | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Match level.** Applitools' name for how strict a comparison is. *Example:* "Layout" compares only the relative positions of elements. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | applitools.com/docs/eyes/concepts/best-practices/match-levels | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Pixel comparison.** Comparing two screenshots pixel by pixel, within a tolerance. *Example:* Playwright's `toHaveScreenshot()`. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | playwright.dev/docs/api/class-pageassertions | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **SSIM (structural similarity).** An image comparison that looks at local patterns rather than single pixels. *Example:* jest-image-snapshot's `ssim` comparison method. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | raw.githubusercontent.com/americanexpress/jest-image-snapshot/main/README.md | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Structured comparison.** Comparing element positions, sizes, computed styles and text read from the DOM, rather than pixels. *Example:* "the title moved 6 pixels right". [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Threshold (tolerance).** How much difference a comparison accepts before it fails. *Example:* Playwright's default colour threshold is 0.2. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | playwright.dev/docs/api/class-pageassertions | accessed 2026-09-27]

## 6. Data visualisation

- **Aspect ratio.** A chart's width divided by its height. *Example:* a wide, flat chart makes slopes look gentler. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Banking to 45 degrees.** Cleveland's advice to choose the aspect ratio so that line slopes average 45 degrees, which a later experiment did not support. *Example:* stretching a chart until its trend lines tilt at about 45 degrees. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | justintalbot.com/research/slope-ratio-comparisons.pdf | 2012]
- **Baseline (of an axis).** The value where an axis starts. *Example:* a bar chart whose value axis starts at zero. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Categorical, sequential and diverging palettes.** Sets of colours for unordered groups, for ordered values from low to high, and for values on either side of a midpoint. *Example:* Atlassian supports only categorical chart palettes today. [VERIFIED | atlassian.design/foundations/color/data-visualization-color | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Chartability.** A set of heuristics for auditing the accessibility of charts. *Example:* its critical test that chart text is at least 12 pixels. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | chartability.github.io/POUR-CAF | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Chartjunk.** Tufte's name for decoration in a chart that carries no information. *Example:* a 3-D effect on flat bars. [PARTIAL | guypursey.com/blog/202001041530-tufte-principles-visual-display-quantitative-information | undated; secondary]
- **Control chart.** A chart that plots a measure over time against lines showing its usual range, so that unusual points stand out. *Example:* Jira's control chart of cycle time. [VERIFIED | support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/view-and-understand-the-control-chart | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Cycle time and lead time.** Cycle time is the time from when work on an item started to when it finished. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | kanbanguides.org/english | version 2025.5] Lead time usually runs from when the item was requested, so it contains the cycle time. [ASSUMPTION: general usage] *Example:* an item started on Monday and finished on Thursday has a cycle time of three days.
- **Data-ink ratio.** Tufte's share of a graphic's ink that shows data. *Example:* removing heavy gridlines raises it. [PARTIAL | guypursey.com/blog/202001041530-tufte-principles-visual-display-quantitative-information | undated; secondary]
- **Direct labelling.** Writing a series' name next to its line or bar instead of in a legend. *Example:* "85th percentile" written at the end of a dashed line. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/axes-grids-and-legends | 2023]
- **Draco.** A system that writes chart design rules as hard and soft constraints and checks chart specifications against them. *Example:* a hard constraint that bars must start at zero. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2019-Draco-InfoVis.pdf | 2019]
- **Dual axis.** A chart with two different y-axes, one on each side. *Example:* revenue on the left and headcount on the right. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Encoding (visual channel).** The visual property that carries a data value. *Example:* position, length, angle, area or colour. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | math.pku.edu.cn/teachers/xirb/Courses/biostatistics/Biostatistics2016/GraphicalPerception_Jasa1984.pdf | 1984]
- **Gridline and tick.** A gridline is a light line across the plot at an axis value; a tick is a short mark on the axis with its label. *Example:* ticks at 0, 5, 10 and 15 days. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Jitter.** Nudging overlapping points slightly apart so that they can be seen. *Example:* spreading five items finished on the same day across a few pixels. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | clauswilke.com/dataviz/overlapping-points.html | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Legend.** A key that explains which colour or shape stands for which series. *Example:* a box listing "Bugs" in red and "Stories" in blue. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Lie factor.** Tufte's ratio of the size of an effect shown in a graphic to the size of the effect in the data; 0.95 to 1.05 is acceptable. *Example:* a 10% rise drawn as a bar twice as tall has a lie factor of 10. [PARTIAL | infovis-wiki.net/wiki/Lie_Factor | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Mark.** The drawn shape that represents a data item. *Example:* each dot in a scatterplot. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Metamorphic test.** A test that changes the input in a known way and checks that the output changes, or stays the same, as expected. *Example:* shuffling the data rows should not change the chart. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/pdf/2001.02316 | 2020]
- **Moving range and XmR chart.** A moving range is the difference between one value and the previous one. An XmR (individuals) chart sets its limits from the average moving range. *Example:* limits at the mean plus or minus 3 × (average moving range ÷ 1.128). [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc322.htm | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Overplotting.** Marks covering each other so that some data cannot be seen. *Example:* twenty items finished on one day drawn as one visible dot. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | clauswilke.com/dataviz/overlapping-points.html | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Percentile.** The value below which a given share of observations fall. *Example:* if the 85th percentile of cycle time is 8 days, 85% of items finished within 8 days. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Rolling average.** An average recalculated over a moving window of recent items, which shows a trend. [ASSUMPTION: general usage] *Example:* Jira's control chart shows a rolling average alongside the overall average. [VERIFIED | support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/view-and-understand-the-control-chart | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Scatterplot.** A chart that places each item as a dot by two values. *Example:* completion date on the horizontal axis and cycle time on the vertical axis. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Service level expectation.** A Kanban forecast with an elapsed time and a probability. *Example:* 85% of items finish in eight days or less. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | kanbanguides.org/english | version 2025.5]
- **Sigma (σ).** The Greek letter used for standard deviation. "3 sigma" means three standard deviations from the mean. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Small multiples.** A grid of small charts with the same axes, one for each group. *Example:* one cycle-time chart per team, side by side. [PARTIAL | edwardtufte.com/notebook/illustration-of-small-multiples-magic-knight-tours | 2003-08-18; contributor, not Tufte]
- **Standard deviation.** A measure of how widely values spread around their mean. [ASSUMPTION: general usage] *Example:* Jira's control chart shows the standard deviation of the items' cycle times, alongside their average. [VERIFIED | support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/view-and-understand-the-control-chart | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Truncated axis.** An axis that starts above zero. *Example:* a y-axis starting at 50% makes small changes look large. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/pdf/1907.02035 | 2020]
- **Vega-Lite.** A declarative language for describing charts, which linters such as VizLinter can check. *Example:* a specification saying "bar mark, x = team, y = count". [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | idvxlab.com/papers/2021VIS_VizLinter_Chen.pdf | 2021]
- **Visualization mirage.** A chart that seems to support a message at a glance, where a closer look would undo it. *Example:* an apparent trend that disappears when the data are resampled. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/pdf/2001.02316 | 2020]
- **Western Electric rules.** Rules for spotting signals on a control chart. *Example:* eight points in a row on one side of the centre line. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc32.htm | accessed 2026-09-27]

## 7. AI evaluation

- **A/B test.** An experiment that shows two versions to different real users and measures which one leads more users to act. *Example:* WiserUI-Bench uses A/B-test winners as ground truth. [VERIFIED | arxiv.org/html/2505.05026v5 | 2026-06-04]
- **Agreement rate.** The share of items on which two judges give the same verdict. *Example:* two experts agreeing on 65 of 100 pairs. [VERIFIED | arxiv.org/html/2510.18560v3 | 2026-03-03]
- **"AI slop".** A name, used in Anthropic's skills blog, for the generic look of designs produced by AI when nothing steers them. *Example:* a purple gradient on white with a default font. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills | 2025-11-12]
- **Blocker, major, minor and ranking.** The four severity levels for rubric lines in `05-template.md`. A candidate that fails a blocker cannot become a finalist; a major failure counts against a candidate when candidates are ranked; a minor one is only reported; and a ranking line never fails a candidate, but is one of the criteria used when two candidates are compared. *Example:* text contrast is a blocker. [ASSUMPTION: my terms in these files]
- **Calibration.** Adjusting a judge until its verdicts match a reference, usually a person's labels. *Example:* Anthropic calibrated its evaluator with few-shot examples and score breakdowns. [VERIFIED | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]
- **Calibration set.** Designs that the person has labelled, used to measure how well the judge agrees with the person. `05-template.md` splits these labelled items into prompt examples, a development set and a test set. *Example:* 60 pairs, each marked with the person's preferred design. [ASSUMPTION: my term in these files]
- **Candidate and finalist.** A candidate is one design the generator produced. A finalist is a candidate shown to the person for the final choice. *Example:* 15 candidates over five iterations, of which 3 finalists are shown. [ASSUMPTION: my terms in these files]
- **Checklist (for a judge).** A set of yes-or-no questions written for one task. *Example:* ArtifactsBench gives its judge a 10-dimension checklist per task. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/html/2507.04952 | 2025-09-29]
- **CLAUDE.md, skill and subagent.** In Claude Code, CLAUDE.md is an instruction file loaded into every session. A skill is a file of instructions loaded only when needed. A subagent is a separate model instance with its own context. *Example:* the design evaluator runs as a subagent that loads the rubric. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | code.claude.com/docs/en/memory, code.claude.com/docs/en/skills and code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices | accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Claude Design.** Anthropic Labs' product for making designs, prototypes and slides, launched on 17 April 2026, which can build a team's design system from its code and files. *Example:* handing a design to Claude Code for implementation. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | anthropic.com/news/claude-design-anthropic-labs | 2026-04-17]
- **Cohen's kappa, Fleiss' kappa and Krippendorff's alpha.** Agreement scores corrected for chance. Cohen's kappa compares two raters, while Fleiss' kappa and Krippendorff's alpha handle more. On all three, 0 means chance-level agreement and 1 means perfect agreement. *Example:* 20 designers reached a Krippendorff's alpha of 0.248 on visual preference. [VERIFIED | arxiv.org/html/2511.20513v1 | 2025-11-25]
- **Comprehension probe.** A check in which a model answers the screen's key question from a screenshot alone, and the harness compares the answer with the fixture data. *Example:* "which item took longest?" answered from the chart image. [ASSUMPTION: my term in these files]
- **Criteria drift.** The finding that people discover and change their evaluation criteria while grading examples. *Example:* adding "labels must not overlap" after seeing the first bad chart. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/html/2404.12272 | 2024-04-18]
- **Critique shadowing.** A practice in which one domain expert gives pass-or-fail verdicts with written critiques, which then become the judge's examples. *Example:* the expert writes why a chart fails, and the judge learns from that note. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge | modified 2026-09-01]
- **Crowdsourcing platform (Mechanical Turk, Prolific).** A website where researchers pay members of the public to take part in online studies. *Example:* Heer and Bostock recruited Mechanical Turk workers. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2010-MTurk-CHI.pdf | 2010]
- **Development set and test set.** The two held-out sets of labelled items in `05-template.md`. The development set is used every time the rubric or the prompt examples change; the test set is used only for a final measurement. *Example:* 40 labelled pairs in each. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Evaluator (judge) and generator.** The generator is the model that makes the design. The evaluator, or judge, is the model or person that decides whether it meets the criteria. *Example:* Anthropic's harness separated the two agents. [VERIFIED | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]
- **Evaluator-optimizer workflow.** Anthropic's name for a loop in which one model generates and another evaluates and feeds back. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents | 2024-12-19] *Example:* Anthropic's design harness ran 5 to 15 iterations of such a loop. [VERIFIED | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]
- **F1 score, precision and recall.** Precision is the share of reported problems that are real, recall is the share of real problems that were reported, and F1 combines the two. *Example:* models found text overflow with an F1 of 20.36%. [VERIFIED | cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/38861 | abstract; accessed 2026-09-27]
- **Few-shot example.** A worked example placed in the prompt to show the model what a good answer looks like. *Example:* a past design with the person's verdict and critique. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices | accessed 2026-09-27]

- **Inverse Constitutional AI.** A method that turns a set of preference choices into a short list of written principles that explain them. *Example:* deriving a principle such as "prefers concise answers" from a group's choices. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/html/2406.06560v2 | 2025-04-21]
- **Jury (panel of judges).** Several different judge models whose verdicts are combined. *Example:* three smaller models from different companies voting. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/html/2404.18796 | 2024-05-01]
- **Layers (universal, platform, taste and purpose).** The three parts of the benchmark in `01-benchmark.md`. The universal layer is the same for every project, the platform layer comes from the project's design system, and the taste and purpose layer comes from the approving person. [ASSUMPTION: my terms in these files]
- **Leniency.** A judge's tendency to pass work it should fail. *Example:* Anthropic's evaluator talked itself into approving work after finding problems. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]

- **LLM-as-a-judge.** Using a large language model, or a multimodal model that also reads images, to grade outputs. *Example:* a model deciding which of two pages is better designed. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Order-stable verdict.** A pairwise verdict that stays the same when the two items are shown in the opposite order. *Example:* "first is better" in one order and "second is better" in the swapped order means the same design won both times. [ASSUMPTION: my term in these files]
- **Pairwise comparison and single-answer grading.** In pairwise comparison the judge picks the better of two items. In single-answer, or pointwise, grading it scores one item alone. *Example:* WebDevJudge found pairwise better by more than 8 points. [VERIFIED | arxiv.org/html/2510.18560v3 | 2026-03-03]
- **Plateau.** The point at which further iterations stop improving scores. *Example:* Anthropic's evaluator scores rose and then levelled off. [VERIFIED | anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps | 2026-03-24]
- **Position bias.** A judge's tendency to prefer the first, or the second, of two items regardless of quality. *Example:* swapping the order changed GPT-4's verdict in 46.3% of one set of comparisons. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/html/2305.17926 | 2023-08-30]
- **Prompt examples.** A few labelled examples placed in the judge's prompt to show it what good verdicts look like. They are kept separate from the held-out set that measures the judge. *Example:* three past designs with the person's verdicts and critiques. [ASSUMPTION: my term in these files]
- **R² (variance explained).** The share of the variation in one measure that a model accounts for, from 0 to 1. *Example:* R² = 0.65 means 65% of the variation in complexity ratings was explained. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Reference-guided grading.** Giving the judge a correct or approved answer to compare against. *Example:* an approved screenshot of the previous release. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/html/2306.05685 | 2023-12-24]
- **Regression guard.** A check kept to catch a problem coming back, even if it rarely fails today. [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Rubric and rubric line.** A rubric is a written list of criteria that a judge applies. A rubric line is one criterion. *Example:* "every chart has a title in real text". [ASSUMPTION: general usage]
- **Self-preference bias.** A judge's tendency to rate its own output higher than others rate it. *Example:* GPT-4 favouring summaries it wrote. [VERIFIED as read by a sub-agent | arxiv.org/html/2404.13076 | 2024-04-15]
- **Taste note and taste log.** A taste note is a written record of one of the approving person's preferences, with a reason, an example and a date. The taste log is the list of them. *Example:* "reference lines are labelled directly, not in a legend". [ASSUMPTION: my terms in these files]

The true negative rate is the share of items that should not be flagged which the judge correctly leaves alone. *Example:* a judge that passes everything has a true negative rate of 100% but catches no failures.
